title: "Sharing Templates"
description: "Every template is private until you share it. Grant access to people, teams or the whole organization at Can use or Can edit level."
---

## Private by default

Every template you create — from scratch, by duplicating, or by capturing an
existing tree — starts **Private**. Nobody else can find it, open it, or apply
it. It does not appear in anyone else's Templates list or Apply picker.

That is deliberate: a half-built template is not something you want colleagues
stumbling into. Share it when it is ready.

Open the share dialog from **Share…** in the builder header, or from the share
icon on the template's row in the Templates list.

![The share dialog for a private template, with only the owner listed and organization access set to No access](/images/templates/share-dialog-private.png)

The dialog always states the current state in plain language at the bottom, and
the badge below it matches the **Audience** badge you see in the list. For a
private template:

> Private. Only you can see this template. Nobody else can find it, open it, or
> apply it — it will not appear in anyone else's Templates list or Apply picker.

## The three sections

**User Access** — named individuals. Search for someone in **Add access** and
they appear here. The author always holds **OWNER** and cannot be removed or
demoted; ownership is not transferable.

**Team Access** — whole teams. Everyone in the team inherits the level you set.

**Organization Access** — everybody in the workspace, in one switch.

## The access levels

Each row (except the owner) carries a level selector with three options.

![The access level dropdown showing No access, Can use and Can edit with their descriptions](/images/templates/share-levels.png)

| Level | What it allows |
| --- | --- |
| **No access** | *Only the people and teams listed above.* Excludes this subject. |
| **Can use** | *View and apply.* They can see the template and instantiate it — but not change it. |
| **Can edit** | *View, apply, edit.* They can also change the template's structure, fields and variables. |

Set **Organization Access** to **Can use** and the summary updates:

![The share dialog with organization access set to Can use and the summary explaining the effect](/images/templates/share-dialog-org.png)

> Anyone in Acme Enterprise can see and apply this template. Only you (or an
> admin) can change it.

That combination — **organization: Can use**, editing kept to yourself — is the
right default for a template you want people to adopt but not fork.

## Three audiences, one badge

The **Audience** column on the Templates list collapses all of the above into
one of three badges:

* **Private** — no grants beyond the owner.
* **Shared** — granted to specific people and/or teams.
* **Organization** — granted to the whole workspace.

## Editing is separate from sharing

Two rules that are easy to conflate:

**Being able to edit a template does not let you re-share it.** Re-sharing is an
**owner-level** action. Somebody with **Can edit** may change the template's
contents but not who else can see it. In practice, the **Share…** control is only
offered to the owner (or an admin with the override permission).

**Being able to see a template does not let you apply it anywhere.** Applying
also requires permission to create workstreams and items **at the target**. A
template cannot be used as a route around item permissions.

## Excluding someone from a broader grant

Grants combine, and an explicit **person-level** entry wins over any team or
organization grant. That is what makes exclusions work:

> Share with the **Engineering** team at *Can use*, then add **Bob** explicitly
> at **No access**. Everyone in Engineering can use the template. Bob cannot —
> even though he is in Engineering, and even if the organization is also
> granted.

The reverse also holds: a person granted **Can edit** directly keeps it even if
their team only has **Can use**.

::alert{type="note" title="Invisible means invisible"}
A template you have not been granted access to does not appear anywhere and
cannot be opened by URL. DevStride does not distinguish "this exists but you
may not see it" from "this does not exist" — that is on purpose, so a template's
existence is not leaked by an error message.
::

## Deleting and duplicating

**Duplicate** (the copy icon on a row) gives you your own private copy of any
template you can see — the usual way to adapt someone else's template without
asking them to change theirs.

**Delete** removes the template. Work already created from it is untouched:
applying is a one-time instantiation, so nothing created from a template depends
on the template continuing to exist.
