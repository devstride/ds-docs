## Where you can start from

Applying always means the same four steps; there are just several doors into
them. Each door pre-selects the location for you.

**From a column in the Map.** The `···` menu on any column header carries
**Apply Template…**, which applies under that column's workstream.

![The Map column menu showing Apply Template, Export as Template and Import Template](/images/templates/entry-point-column-menu.png)

**From the add button.** The dropdown arrow beside an add-item button offers
**From template…** — *Instantiate a saved tree of workstreams and items here.*

![The add-item dropdown offering Create Items with AI and From template](/images/templates/entry-point-add-item.png)

**From any item.** Right-click a workstream or item and choose **Apply
Template…** to place the template underneath it.

![The item context menu showing Apply Template and Save as Template](/images/templates/entry-point-item-menu.png)

**From the builder.** The **Preview** button on a saved template opens the same
dialog, so you can try a template out while you are writing it.

::alert{type="note" title="Apply Template… is not Import Template"}
**Import Template** and **Export as Template** in the column menu are the older
spreadsheet import/export. They are unrelated to this feature and unchanged.
::

## Step 1 — Location and template

The dialog opens on **Template**. The bar at the top names where the template
will land — *Applying into workstream Product* — and is clickable if you want to
point somewhere else.

![Step 1 of the apply dialog listing four templates with their audience, rooting, variable count and health](/images/templates/apply-step1.png)

Each card shows the template's audience, what it **starts with**, how many
variables it will ask for, its **health**, and its description. The footer keeps
score: *4 of 4 templates can be applied here.*

Templates that don't fit the chosen location are hidden. Tick **Show templates
that don't fit here** to see them anyway — each one then explains why it cannot
be used, rather than silently vanishing.

::alert{type="tip" title="How “fit” is decided"}
The fit rule is the one from the template's **Applies to** column. A
Workstream-rooted template can be placed under any workstream. A Feature-rooted
one can only be placed under a workstream or an Epic, because that is where a
Feature is allowed to live in your work-type ladder.
::

Pick a template and click **Next**.

## Step 2 — Variables

The template's questions, in an order that respects their dependencies: a team
variable is always asked before the user variable scoped to it.

![Step 2 showing six empty variable fields with labels, required markers and hints](/images/templates/apply-step2-empty.png)

Each field shows its **label**, a red asterisk if required, and a hint naming the
underlying variable and type — *Variable go_live (date).* Optional fields say so:
*Optional — leave blank to skip it.*

The fields react to each other as you fill them in. Choose **Onboarding team =
Service Team** and the **Customer success manager** picker immediately narrows:

![The assignee picker filtered to Service Team members](/images/templates/apply-scoped-assignee.png)

> Showing 2 of 98 — Service Team members eligible for this slot.

An **Options** variable renders as a dropdown of exactly the values the template
author defined:

![The Training tier dropdown offering Standard, Premium and Enterprise](/images/templates/apply-choice-options.png)

Filled in, the form is short — six answers for an eighteen-item programme:

![Step 2 fully filled: Service Team, Maya Morgan, Nov 16, Northwind Trading, Premium, 250](/images/templates/apply-step2-filled.png)

Click **Preview**.

## Step 3 — Preview

Nothing has been created yet. This step shows exactly what *will* be.

![Step 3 preview showing 18 items with resolved titles and date ranges](/images/templates/apply-step3-preview.png)

Read it top to bottom and check the template did what you expected:

* **18 items will be created · start dates anchored to Nov 16** — the count and
  the date anchor.
* Every title is **resolved**. `{{customer_name}} Onboarding` has become
  **Northwind Trading Onboarding**; `{{training_tier}} training` has become
  **Premium training**.
* Every date range is **computed** from the anchor — the workstream runs
  *Oct 2 → Nov 21*, kickoff *Oct 2 → Oct 12*, go-live *Nov 16 → Nov 21*.
* Assignee avatars show who each item will land on.
* Warnings appear inline. *Assigning items to a person grants them view and edit
  access to those items* is the common one — a consequence of assigning, not a
  problem, but worth knowing before you create eighteen of them.

If something is wrong, **Back** returns you to the variables with your answers
intact.

## Step 4 — Apply

**Apply Template** starts the job. The dialog switches to a live checklist:
every node in the tree, ticked off as it is created, above a running counter
(*0 of 18 items created*). You can leave it running and close the dialog — the
job continues, and the toast centre reports the outcome.

Creation is **not** all-or-nothing. Nodes are created parent-first, and if one
fails, its subtree is skipped while its siblings carry on. The job then finishes
as *completed with errors* and lists exactly which nodes failed and why, so you
can fix those by hand rather than unpicking a half-built tree.

Once it finishes, the items are ordinary DevStride items. Nothing links them
back to the template.

## When a template cannot be applied

### It doesn't fit here

The template's root type isn't allowed under the location you chose. Pick a
different location, or start the apply from a place where it fits — the *N of M
templates can be applied here* counter in step 1 tells you before you commit.

### A required variable is blank

Apply is blocked until every required variable has an answer. Optional ones may
be left blank; the fields bound to them are left unset.

### The template needs attention

A template's **health** is recomputed against your current configuration every
time it is read, and shown as a badge on the Templates list and on each card in
step 1.

* **Healthy** (green tick) — every reference resolves.
* **Needs attention** (amber warning) — something the template points at has been
  archived or deactivated: a work type, a team, a board, a board folder, or a
  member. The badge's tooltip and the banner in the builder list each broken
  reference and where it sits in the tree.

A template that needs attention **cannot be applied** until the reference is
restored. The banner is explicit that nothing was removed from your template —
archiving is reversible, and unarchiving heals it.

::alert{type="note" title="Deletions are handled differently from archiving"}
Archiving is reversible, so DevStride **flags** it and waits. Deleting is not —
when a custom field, a custom-field option, a tag or a priority is deleted, it is
**stripped** from any template that referenced it, and the template records a
small grey note in the node inspector saying what happened. Those notes never
block applying; there is nothing left to fix.
::

### No open cycle

A node whose **Board** is set to **Cycle** resolves at apply time to whichever
board is currently open on the chosen cadence. If no cycle is open, apply stops
with *No open cycle is available for the selected cadence/folder* rather than
dropping the work onto an arbitrary board. Open the next cycle on that cadence,
or change the node's board mode.

### You lack permission at the target

Seeing a template is not enough. You also need permission to create workstreams
and items where you are applying it. This is re-checked when the job runs, not
just when the dialog opens.

## A note on scale

A template may hold up to **500 nodes** and nest **10 levels**. Applying a large
one creates a lot of work at once — and, if the nodes are assigned, grants a lot
of people access to a lot of items at once. The preview step exists precisely so
that is a decision rather than a surprise.
