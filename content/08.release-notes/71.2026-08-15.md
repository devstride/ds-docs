## 🚀 Sign up with Google or Microsoft

Google and Microsoft used to be an *alternate way to sign in* to an account you had already created with a password. Now they're a way to **create the account in the first place**.

On the sign-up page, accept the terms and choose **Sign up with Google** or **Sign up with Microsoft**. You authenticate with that provider and come straight back with your account made — never choosing a DevStride password at all.

- **One person, one account.** If the address already belongs to a DevStride account, you're simply signed in to it and the provider is linked for next time. Signing up with Google doesn't quietly create a second you.
- **The buttons wait for your agreement.** They stay greyed out until you've accepted the terms of use and privacy policy — choosing a provider sends you straight to them, so your agreement is recorded before you leave.
- **You still need an email address.** It's where notifications go and how invitations find you, so sign-up completes only if your provider tells us your address. Nearly every Google and personal Microsoft account does; if yours doesn't, you're pointed at email-and-password sign-up rather than left with a failure to interpret.
- **Only configured providers appear.** If your provider has no button, sign up with an email address and password.

Email-and-password sign-up is completely unchanged, and so is signing *in* — with a password, with a social provider, or through enterprise SSO.

📚 [Creating your account](https://docs.devstride.com/getting-started/creating-your-account)

::alert{type="info" title="Work accounts and single sign-on"}
If your organization requires single sign-on, you can't create a *new* account with your work identity — that route stays reserved for people an administrator has invited. Ask for an invitation; signing in with SSO then works normally.
::
