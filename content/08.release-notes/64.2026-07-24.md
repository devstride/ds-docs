## 🛎️ A clearer Service Desk off-switch, and more portal foundation

This release rounds out the customer support portal's access and identity layer — the secure backbone the in-portal request experience will use — and makes turning Service Desk off do exactly what it now says it does.

### 🔌 Disabling Service Desk clearly takes the portal offline

The **Disable Service Desk** confirmation now spells out what happens, and disabling follows through on it: your **customer support portal goes offline immediately**, customers can't open it or sign in, anyone already signed in is turned away on their next action, and any pending sign-in links stop working. Your internal tickets, email intake, requesters, companies, and every company's portal settings (web address, access mode, branding) are **preserved** — so re-enabling brings the portal back exactly as it was.

In short, turning Service Desk off is now a real, reversible "portal offline" switch, not just a hidden module.

📚 Learn more: [**Enabling Service Desk**](/major-modules/service-desk/enabling-service-desk)

### 🔐 More of the portal access foundation

Behind the scenes, the portal's per-company access controls and the secure read layer that serves each customer only their own company's data landed in this release. This is foundation that the in-portal request experience (browsing and managing your requests) builds on — that experience is still rolling out next and isn't part of this release. What you can set up today is unchanged: a company's portal web address, [access mode](/major-modules/service-desk/customer-support-portal#who-can-sign-in-access-modes), and branding, all on the company record under **Service Desk → Companies**.

### 🧱 Reliability hardening

A batch of behind-the-scenes durability and observability improvements to how DevStride processes background events — nothing to configure, aimed at making sure work items, rollups, and automations stay consistent under load. No action needed on your part.
