## 📚 Knowledge Base

The Knowledge Base is a place to house information that doesn't belong on an assignable item — **documentation, FAQs, process details**. That knowledge outlives any one story, and until now it lived in someone's notes app.

Write an article once, file it in a category, and decide who reads it.

- **Internal only, or shared with customers.** Every article carries an audience. Keep it **Internal** and only your team ever sees it. Mark it **Portal** and it appears in the **Help center** on that customer's company Service Desk portal — the same place they track their requests.
- **Categories organize it, and control it.** Build a tree of categories to give readers something to browse. Mark a category **Internal** and everything nested beneath it is hidden from customers — the whole section, however deep, including articles you add later. Set it back and everything returns exactly as it was, because nothing is rewritten along the way.
- **Narrow it to particular customers.** A customer-facing article can go to every company, or to specific ones you choose.
- **Your team sees the whole picture.** The **Library** shows members everything published, internal and customer-facing alike, with a badge marking which is which. One search covers the internal runbook and the customer guide.
- **It answers questions before they become tickets.** When a customer is describing a problem on a request form, matching articles surface right there under *"These articles may help."* The best support ticket is the one nobody needed to file.
- **Published means published.** Articles get a permanent URL when you publish, and renaming an article never breaks a link you've shared.

**Every member can read it** — the Library needs no permission at all. Writing and publishing are gated: grant the **Manage knowledge base** role template to the people who author your documentation.
::

## 📊 Time Allocation Matrix

A new report under **Analyze → Analytics → Time** that answers a question the others don't: *where did the effort actually go?*

Instead of slicing logged time by person, it rolls it up through **your own hierarchy** — your epics, your stories, whatever structure you use — and spreads it across calendar months. Read down the column for how effort divided across your work; read across a row for how one branch moved over time. Subtotals and percentage allocations are computed for you.

- **You define the rows.** Choose which item types to nest and where to look. Change the levels and the same time is re-cut a completely different way.
- **Built for quarter-end.** Capitalization reporting, client billing summaries, and R&D allocation all fall out of the same report.
- **Export to Excel** with every row, proper outlining, and a provenance line recording exactly when the snapshot was taken and over what range — so it's safe to hand to finance.
- **Save it as a view** and re-run it next month with new dates.
