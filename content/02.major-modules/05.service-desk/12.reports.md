The **Reports** tab in Service Desk answers the questions support managers actually get asked: are we keeping up, how fast do we reply, are we hitting our promises, and who is carrying the load. Six reports cover it, each with its own date range, grouping, and CSV export.

You'll find it as the **Reports** tab alongside Inbox, Suspended, Requesters, and Companies.

::alert{type="info" title="Reports read your real tickets"}
Nothing here needs setting up. The reports read the tickets you already have, so they start showing data as soon as Service Desk is handling requests. Reports that depend on SLA policies will look empty until you've [set some up](/major-modules/service-desk/sla).
::

## The six reports

| Report | What it answers |
| --- | --- |
| **Created vs Resolved** | Are we keeping up? Tickets opened against tickets closed over time, with the running **Backlog** alongside. |
| **First Response Time** | How long does a customer wait for a first human reply? Shown as **Median**, **P90**, and **Average**. |
| **Time to Resolution** | How long does a ticket take to actually finish, from arrival to resolution. |
| **SLA Attainment** | What percentage of tickets met the promise you made, for tickets covered by an SLA policy. |
| **Agent Activity** | Who is doing the work — **Replies sent** and **Tickets resolved** per agent. |
| **First Contact Resolution** | How often a ticket is solved without a second round trip. |

::alert{type="tip" title="Median and P90 beat the average"}
On the response and resolution reports, the **average** is easily dragged around by one ticket that sat over a weekend. The **median** tells you what a typical customer experienced, and **P90** tells you what your slowest tenth experienced — usually the more useful number when you're deciding whether to change staffing.
::

## Shaping a report

Each report has the same controls above it:

- **From** and **To** set the date range.
- **Day**, **Week**, or **Month** sets how time-series data is bucketed. Use Day for a busy week, Month for a year in review.
- **Group by** splits the data into series. Choose **None**, **Channel**, **Company**, **Work Type**, **Priority**, or **Agent**.
- **SLA-policied only** restricts the report to tickets actually covered by an SLA policy. Leave it off to see everything.

Grouping is where these earn their keep. *First Response Time* grouped by **Company** shows which customers are waiting longest; grouped by **Channel** it shows whether email requests get slower answers than portal ones.

If a report shows **No data for the selected period**, widen the date range or clear the grouping — a narrow group can easily have no tickets in a short window.

## Exporting to CSV

Every report exports. Click **Export CSV** and DevStride confirms:

> Export queued — find the CSV in the analytics exports list.

The export is **queued as a background job** rather than downloaded on the spot, so a large date range doesn't tie up your browser. Collect the finished file from the analytics exports list. The CSV honours the filters you have applied — date range, bucketing, grouping, and the SLA-policied-only toggle — so what you download matches what you were looking at.

## Who can see Reports

Viewing reports needs the **Read report throughput** permission, which ships on the **Service Desk Agent** role template. Anyone already using that template picks it up automatically; for a custom role, grant the permission to add the Reports tab. See [Service Desk permissions](/major-modules/service-desk/permissions).

## Prioritising with customer signal

Two ticket fields feed the judgement calls these reports surface, and both are set by your team rather than the customer:

- **Customer Rank** — where this request sits in that customer's own ordering of what they want from you.
- **Importance** — how much it matters to them: **Critical**, **Important**, or **Nice to have**. Use **Clear** to remove the value.

Both can be set inline on a ticket or applied in bulk across a filtered set, and both are available as columns and filters when [finding and filtering tickets](/major-modules/service-desk/finding-and-filtering-tickets). They record what the *customer* considers urgent, which is often not the same as your internal priority — keeping the two separate means you can see where they disagree.
