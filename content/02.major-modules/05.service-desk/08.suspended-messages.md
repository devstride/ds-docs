---
title: "The Suspended Queue"
description: "Some inbound mail is held back for safety instead of becoming a ticket. The Suspended tab is where you review what was held and why, and the Dropped mail tab lists every email that didn't become a ticket."
---

Not every email that reaches your support address should become a ticket. Auto-replies, spam, and messages that fail their sender checks would clutter your Inbox — or worse, create noise your team has to clean up. DevStride holds these back in the **Suspended** queue so a person can decide what to do with them.

::alert{type="info" title="Who sees it"}
The **Suspended** tab requires the **Manage Requesters** permission. Agents focused only on replying to customers won't see it. See [Permissions](/major-modules/service-desk/permissions).
::

## What lands here

Open **Service Desk → Suspended** for a table of held messages, showing **From**, **Subject**, **Reason**, and **Received**. The **Reason** explains why each message was held:

| Reason | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Auto-response** | The message looks like an automatic reply, such as an out-of-office message or a recognized delivery-failure notice from a mail server. These messages are held instead of opening tickets. |
| **Rate limited** | The sender sent an unusually high volume in a short time. |
| **Spam** | The message was flagged as likely spam. |
| **SPF failed / DKIM failed / DMARC failed** | The message failed an email authenticity check, meaning the sender's identity couldn't be verified. |
| **Blocked sender** | The message came from a requester you've [blocked](/major-modules/service-desk/requesters-and-companies). |

![The Suspended tab. When no mail is held, it shows a clean empty state; held messages appear here with From, Subject, Reason, and Received columns for triage.](/images/service-desk/suspended.png)

::alert{type="note" title="Some mail is dropped outright"}
A message carrying a virus, or an exact duplicate of one already processed, is discarded automatically and never appears in this queue — there's nothing useful to look at. That includes the same email resent within 15 minutes by a mail program that leaves out the message's identifier: it joins the first ticket instead of opening a second one. The Suspended queue is only for messages a human might legitimately want to review; the [Dropped mail](#the-dropped-mail-tab) tab lists everything that didn't become a ticket, including these.
::

## Reviewing what's held

Click a held message to open it. You'll see who sent it, its subject, why it was held, and a text preview of up to 280 characters. This preview is not the full original email.

Automations that run when a message is held also apply to recognized delivery-failure notices. If you use those automations to alert staff, expect them to include these notices too.

Two actions are available:

- **Mark spam** — record it as spam and clear it from the queue.
- **Delete** — discard it.

::alert{type="note" title="Releasing a held message isn't available yet"}
**Release** and **View original** appear in the dialog but are switched off. Both need the retained copy of the original message, which is still being built — releasing has to re-run the message through the pipeline to create the ticket, and doing it any other way would take a customer's message out of triage having created nothing.

So a legitimate message that was held cannot be turned into a ticket from this screen today. Ask the sender to resend it, or fix whatever held it — usually their own domain's sender setup — so their next message comes straight through.
::

::alert{type="tip" title="Make it a habit"}
Skim the Suspended queue periodically so a real request that tripped a filter doesn't sit unanswered — it matters more while releasing isn't available, because a held message needs a person to notice it and follow up another way. If a particular trusted customer keeps getting held, fixing their sender setup (or confirming they're not blocked) usually stops it.
::

## The Dropped mail tab

Every email that reaches one of your support addresses is recorded, and DevStride keeps track of what happened to it: it became a ticket, it was held for review, or it was turned away. **Service Desk → Dropped mail** lists the ones that did **not** become a ticket, newest first, 100 at a time — click **Load more** at the bottom for older ones. Like the Suspended tab, it requires the **Manage Requesters** permission.

Each row shows:

- **To** — which of your support addresses the email was sent to.
- **What happened** — **Turned away**, **Held for review** (it's also in the Suspended queue), or **Failing, being retried** when DevStride hit an error it keeps retrying.
- **Reason** — why, for example a virus, spam, a blocked sender, or a duplicate. **Truncated** appears beside the reason when the email was longer than DevStride can store.
- **Received** — when the email arrived.
- **Original kept until** — how long the original message is kept (395 days after it arrived). It reads **Deleted** when the sender had asked to be erased, because their original is deleted straight away.

The list deliberately never shows who sent the email or its subject. It answers "did this email reach us, and what happened to it?" without putting a customer's details on another screen. To look up a particular customer's message, search your tickets or the Suspended queue.

::alert{type="info" title="Mail is also counted every day"}
Once a day DevStride compares the number of emails the mail service accepted for your support addresses with the number it recorded. If any went missing in between, DevStride's own on-call team is alerted, so a delivery problem is found even when nobody thinks to look.
::
