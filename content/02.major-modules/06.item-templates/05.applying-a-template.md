description: "Pick a place, answer the template's questions, check exactly what will be created, then create it. Four steps, with a real preview before anything is written."

Applying is where a template becomes actual work. It's a four-step wizard, and step three shows you precisely what you're about to get before anything exists.

## Where to start from

**Apply Template…** appears wherever it makes sense to add work:

- the **+** dropdown at the top of a column in **Map Value** (shown as **From template…**)
- the **right-click menu** on any workstream or work item
- the **Add item** dropdown

![The column add-dropdown in Map Value offering From template…](/images/item-templates/apply-entry-map.png)

Starting from a specific item pre-fills the target for you. Starting from the top of a column doesn't, so you pick the location in step 1.

## Step 1 — Choose the location and the template

![Step 1 of the apply dialog, listing every template with its audience, root type and health.](/images/item-templates/apply-step1-choose.png)

The **location** is the parent the template will be created under. If it isn't already set, choose one:

![The location picker browsing the workstream tree.](/images/item-templates/apply-choose-location.png)

Once a location is chosen, the list re-sorts itself around it. Templates that fit are listed normally; templates that don't are hidden, with a count at the bottom — *"4 of 4 templates can be applied here"* — and a **Show templates that don't fit here** checkbox if you want to see the rest anyway.

![Step 1 with a location selected, showing which templates fit.](/images/item-templates/apply-step1-with-location.png)

"Fitting" is a hierarchy question. A template that starts with a Feature can be applied under a Workstream or an Epic, because those are legal parents of a Feature. It cannot be applied under a Story.

Each card shows the template's **audience**, what it **starts with**, how many **variables** it asks for, and a **health** badge. A template flagged as broken can't be applied until its references are repaired — see [Keeping templates healthy](/major-modules/item-templates/keeping-templates-healthy).

## Step 2 — Fill in the values

![Step 2 asking for the template's variables.](/images/item-templates/apply-step2-variables.png)

The template's [variables](/major-modules/item-templates/variables) appear as a form. Required ones are marked with a red asterisk and block the **Preview** button until they're filled.

Questions appear in dependency order, so a **User** variable scoped to a team is always asked *after* the team it depends on — and its person picker offers only that team's members.

Templates with no variables skip this step entirely.

## Step 3 — Preview

![Step 3 showing exactly the tree that will be created.](/images/item-templates/apply-step3-preview.png)

> *This is exactly what will be created. Nothing exists yet.*

The preview is a genuine dry run against your organization, not a mock-up. It resolves every token, every date offset and every reference, tells you how many items will be created, and surfaces any problem that would block the apply — a required value still missing, an assignee who isn't in the scoped team, a cadence with no open cycle, an invalid placement.

Some findings are warnings rather than blockers. A date clamped to fit inside a cycle, for instance, is reported but doesn't stop you.

**Read this screen.** It's the cheapest moment to notice that a template is landing in the wrong place.

## Step 4 — Apply

![Step 4 confirming five of five items created.](/images/item-templates/apply-step4-result.png)

**Apply Template** creates the tree. Nodes are created parent-first, and progress ticks through as it goes — bigger templates take a few seconds.

**Go to created work** takes you straight to the new root item. Your new items are ordinary items from this moment on: no ongoing link to the template, no lock, nothing special about them.

![The applied tree in Map Value — a new workstream with its three epics.](/images/item-templates/applied-result-map.png)

::alert{type="warning" title="A partial apply is possible"}
Creating the tree is not all-or-nothing. If one node can't be created — usually a permission or configuration problem specific to that item — its own subtree is skipped and its **siblings still get created**. The result screen reports what succeeded and what didn't, so a run can finish with some items created and some not. This is why step 3 exists: previewing catches most of these before anything is written.
::

## Applying and permissions

Applying a template needs two things: **access to the template** (it's yours, shared with you, or shared with the organization) and the ordinary permission to **create items in the target location**. A template is not a route around item permissions — it creates items as you, and every check that applies to creating an item by hand applies here too.
