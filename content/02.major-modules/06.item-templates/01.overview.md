Some work has the same shape every time. Onboarding a new client. Launching a product. Running a compliance review. The names change, the dates change, the people change — but the **structure** doesn't. The same workstream, the same epics under it, the same checklist of stories.

A **template** captures that structure once so you never have to rebuild it by hand.

![The Templates page listing the organization's saved templates, with their audience, owner, starting item type, and variable count.](/images/item-templates/templates-list.png)

## What a template actually is

Think of a template as a **cookie cutter for work items**.

You draw the shape once — a workstream, the epics under it, the features and stories under those — and you save it. Later, when the same job comes round again, you pick the template, answer a couple of questions ("which client?", "what's the launch date?"), and DevStride creates the whole tree for you in one go, correctly nested, with the fields already filled in.

Three ideas make up everything in this section:

| Idea | In one sentence |
| --- | --- |
| **Node** | One item in the shape — a workstream, an epic, a story. The template is a tree of nodes. |
| **Variable** | A question the template asks when you use it, so one template serves many situations. |
| **Apply** | Using the template: pick a place in your map, answer the questions, and the real items get created. |

A template is a **blueprint, not a copy**. Nothing exists until you apply it, and applying it twice gives you two independent sets of items — changing the template afterwards does not reach back and change work that was already created from it.

## What a node can carry

Each node in the template is one future item. Beyond its title, a node can carry almost everything an item carries: a description, start and due dates, a team, an assignee, a board or cycle placement, a priority, tags, an effort estimate, and any custom fields configured for that work type.

You are not limited to fixed values. Any of those fields can instead be **wired to a variable** — see [Variables](/major-modules/item-templates/variables) — so the template asks for it at apply time rather than hard-coding it.

::alert{type="info" title="Templates point at your real configuration"}
A template doesn't invent its own work types, custom fields, priorities, tags, teams or boards — it **references the ones your organization already has**. That's why a template built by one person behaves identically for everyone else, and it's also why templates need a little care when you change that configuration later. See [Keeping templates healthy](/major-modules/item-templates/keeping-templates-healthy).
::

## What a template starts with

Every template has a **root type**, shown on the list page as **Starts with**. It is either:

- a **Workstream** — the template creates a workstream (and everything beneath it), so it's applied into a workstream or folder; or
- a **work item** of a specific type — Enterprise, Epic, Feature, Story, and so on — so it's applied under an item that is allowed to be that type's parent.

The **Applies to** column tells you the flip side: which kinds of parent will accept this template. A template that starts with a Feature can be dropped under a Workstream or an Epic, because those are the legal parents of a Feature in that organization's hierarchy.

This is why the apply picker greys out templates that don't fit where you're standing — it is reading your work-type hierarchy, not guessing.

## Where templates live

**Templates** is its own item in the left sidebar, under **Admin**. The page lists every template you can see, with four tabs:

- **All** — everything visible to you
- **Mine** — templates you authored
- **Shared with me** — templates someone shared with you or with a team you're in
- **Organization** — templates shared with the whole organization

Each row shows the template's audience, owner, root type, what it can be applied to, and how many variables it asks for. The row actions are **Share**, **Duplicate**, **Edit** and **Delete**.

::alert{type="warning" title="Deleting a template is permanent"}
There is no archive for templates and no undo. Deleting one does **not** touch items that were already created from it — those are ordinary items and carry on as normal. If you only want to take a template out of circulation, set its audience back to Private instead of deleting it.
::

## Two ways to get a template

You don't have to draw a template from a blank page. There are two routes, and most people use the second one first:

1. **Build it** — start from **New Template** and lay out the tree yourself. See [Building a template](/major-modules/item-templates/building-a-template).
2. **Capture it** — find a piece of work in your map that already has the right shape, and turn it into a template in two clicks with **Save as Template…**. See [Capturing an existing item as a template](/major-modules/item-templates/capturing-a-template).

Capturing is usually faster: you get a template with every value copied as a fixed value, and then you open the builder and turn just the handful that should vary into variables.

## Who can do what

Templates use two layers of access, and both have to allow an action before it happens.

**Role permissions** decide what you can do at all. They live in **Settings → Permissions**, under **ItemTemplate**:

| Permission | What it grants |
| --- | --- |
| **Access Templates** | See the Templates module in the sidebar. Ships on the default Member role. |
| **Create Item Template** | Create new templates. |
| **Manage Own Item Templates** | Update and delete templates you authored. |
| **Manage All Item Templates** | Update and delete other members' templates — an admin override. |

![The ItemTemplate permission group in Settings → Permissions, showing Create, Manage Own and Manage All Item Templates.](/images/item-templates/permissions.png)

**Sharing** decides which specific templates you can see and use. That's per template, set by its owner, and covered in [Sharing templates](/major-modules/item-templates/sharing-templates).

Applying a template also requires the ordinary permission to **create items** in the place you're applying it — a template is not a way around item permissions.
