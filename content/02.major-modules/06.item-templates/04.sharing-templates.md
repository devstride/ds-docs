---
title: "Sharing Templates"
description: "Every template starts private. Share it with named people, whole teams, or the entire organization — and revoke a single person without unpicking the rest."
---

A template you create is **private to you**. Nobody else can find it, open it, or apply it — it doesn't appear in anyone else's Templates list or in their apply picker. Sharing is a separate, deliberate step.

Open it from the **Share** icon on any template row, or from the **Share…** button in the builder header.

![The Share dialog with sections for user access, team access and organization access.](/images/item-templates/share-dialog.png)

## Three layers of access

The dialog has three sections, and they stack: a person gets the **most permissive** grant that applies to them, except where an explicit **No access** entry overrides it.

**User access** — named individuals. Search for someone and give them a level. You appear here as **Owner** and cannot be removed; ownership can't be transferred.

**Team access** — a whole team at once. Everyone in the team inherits the level, and the grant follows team membership, so someone joining the team next month gets access automatically.

**Organization access** — one setting for everybody in the organization.

![The organization access dropdown offering No access, Can use and Can edit.](/images/item-templates/share-dialog-org-roles.png)

## The three levels

| Level | Can see it | Can apply it | Can edit it | Can re-share it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Can use** (Viewer) | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |
| **Can edit** (Editor) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ |
| **Owner** | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |

**Can use** is the right level for almost everyone. People find the template in the apply picker and use it; they can't change it underneath you.

**Can edit** is for co-maintainers. Note the last column: an editor can change the template's structure but **cannot change who it's shared with**. Sharing stays with the owner.

::alert{type="note" title="Revoking one person"}
Setting a specific person to **No access** is an explicit revoke, and it beats a grant they'd otherwise get from a team or from the organization-wide setting. That's how you express "share with the whole Engineering team, except one person" without dismantling the team grant.
::

## The audience badge

Everything above collapses into a single badge shown on the list page and in the builder header:

| Badge | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Private** | Only you. |
| **Shared** | Shared with specific people or teams, but not the whole organization. |
| **Organization** | Shared with everyone in the organization. |

The dialog also spells out the current state in plain English above the buttons, so you can confirm what you're about to do before you save it.

![Organization access set to Can use, with the summary line updating to match.](/images/item-templates/share-dialog-org-can-use.png)

## How sharing and role permissions interact

Sharing decides **which templates** you can see. Role permissions decide **what you're allowed to do at all**. Both must allow an action.

That means a member without **Manage Own Item Templates** cannot edit a template even when it's shared with them as an editor — and DevStride hides the edit affordance rather than showing a button that fails. Conversely, an admin holding **Manage All Item Templates** can edit any template in the organization regardless of how it's shared.

See [Templates overview](/major-modules/item-templates/overview#who-can-do-what) for the full permission list.

## Duplicating instead of sharing

If someone wants their own variant of an organization template rather than access to yours, **Duplicate** on the template row is the better move. It creates an independent copy owned by them, private at birth, which they can edit freely without affecting the original.
