---
title: "Keeping Templates Healthy"
description: "Templates point at your real configuration, so changing that configuration can affect them. Here's what DevStride warns you about, and what it does automatically."
---

A template doesn't own its work types, custom fields, priorities, tags, teams, boards or people — it **references** the ones your organization already has. That's what makes templates consistent. It also means that deleting or archiving one of those records can leave a template pointing at something that no longer exists.

DevStride handles this from both ends: it warns you *before* you change the configuration, and it flags the affected templates *afterwards*.

## The health badge

Every template carries a health status, shown on the list page and in the apply picker.

**Healthy** — every reference resolves. The template is good to apply.

**Needs attention / broken** — something it points at is missing or unusable. A broken template **cannot be applied** until it's fixed; the apply picker shows the state on the card so nobody discovers it halfway through a wizard.

In the builder, the same information appears as a red **problems** badge above the panes. Hover it and each problem names the field and the node it belongs to:

![The problems badge listing three nodes whose cadence has no open cycle.](/images/item-templates/builder-health-problems.png)

Common problems:

| Problem | Usual cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No open cycle for the selected cadence/folder | The cadence has no current cycle, or the folder was archived | Reopen or re-point the cadence, or switch the node's board mode |
| Board field incomplete | Cycle mode selected but no cadence chosen | Open the node and finish the field |
| Missing work type / team / member | The referenced record was archived or deactivated | Restore it, or edit the node to use a live one |
| Variable referenced but not defined | A variable was deleted while nodes still used it | Re-add the variable, or set those fields back to Fixed |

## Before you delete or archive: the warning

When you delete or archive a piece of organization configuration, DevStride checks which templates use it first and tells you in the confirmation dialog — *before* you commit.

The warning is careful about two things.

**It uses the right verb**, because the two kinds of change do genuinely different things:

| What you're doing | Records | Effect on templates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Deleting** (irreversible) | Custom field, custom-field option, tag, priority | The reference is **removed** from every template that used it |
| **Archiving or deactivating** (reversible) | Work type, team, board, board folder, member | Templates are **flagged** for attention — nothing is removed, and restoring the record clears the flag |

**It counts honestly but discloses selectively.** The count is the true organization-wide total. Templates you're allowed to see are named; private templates belonging to other members are counted but shown only as *"N private template(s) belonging to another member"*. So the number can be larger than the list — by design.

## After the change

Templates flagged by an archive keep working for everything else; only the affected node needs attention. Open the template, look at the problems badge, and either restore the archived record or point that node at something live.

Nothing silently deletes a template, and nothing silently changes what one creates.

## Good habits

- **Prefer Cycle mode over a named board.** A node pointing at a specific cycle goes stale when that cycle closes; a node pointing at a *cadence* keeps resolving.
- **Prefer a team to a named person** where the work is really "the delivery team's lead" rather than one individual — teams outlive individual membership.
- **Check the org-wide templates after a configuration change.** If you've just retired a work type or a custom field, the Templates list is the fastest place to see what it touched.
- **Fix templates at the source.** Repair the template, not the items created from it — items already created are independent and unaffected.
