---
title: "Sharing templates"
description: "Templates start private. Share one with specific people, teams, or the whole organization — and use an explicit exclusion when a team grant reaches someone it shouldn't."
---

Every template starts **Private — only you**. Nobody else can find it, open it, or apply it; it won't appear in anyone else's Templates list or apply picker.

Share it when it's ready, from the **Share…** action on the template's row or in the builder header.

## Access levels

| Level | What it allows |
| --- | --- |
| **Can use** | View and apply the template |
| **Can edit** | View, apply, and edit it |
| **Exclude** | Blocked, even if a team or the organization grants access |
| **Remove from list** | Drop this entry and fall back to team or organization access |

You can grant to **individual people**, **teams**, or **the whole organization**.

::alert{type="note" title="Exclude and Remove are not the same"}
**Remove from list** deletes the entry, after which the person falls back to whatever their team or the organization gives them. **Exclude** is an active block that survives those grants. Use Remove to undo a mistake; use Exclude to carve one person out of a team that should otherwise have access.
::

## How access is worked out

When several rules could apply to one person, DevStride settles it in this order:

1. **Their own entry wins** — whatever it says, including Exclude.
2. Otherwise, **an Exclude on any of their teams** wins.
3. Otherwise, **the best level any of their teams grants**.
4. Otherwise, **the organization level**.

The share dialog spells out the consequences inline. If you exclude someone who is on a team you've granted access, the row tells you the person-level exclusion wins and they won't see the template.

At the bottom, a plain-English summary states the effective audience — who can use it, who can also edit it, and who can change sharing.

## Who can change sharing or delete

Only the **owner** — the person who created it — or an administrator holding **MANAGE_ALL_ITEM_TEMPLATES**.

Notably, **Can edit** does *not* include sharing or deleting. Someone can improve your template's content without being able to change who sees it or remove it.

::alert{type="info" title="Ownership stays with the author"}
There's no way to hand a template to someone else. The author remains the owner, and their own access is always re-asserted when sharing is saved — you cannot accidentally lock yourself out of your own template. If ownership genuinely needs to move, have the new owner **Duplicate** it: the copy is theirs.
::

## Duplicating instead of editing

Anyone who can see a template can **Duplicate** it, provided they can create templates at all. The copy is named `<name> (copy)`, is owned by them, and starts private.

This is the intended path when you want to adapt someone else's template. Opening a template you can't edit gives you a read-only builder with **Duplicate to edit** right there.

## Reading the audience

The **Audience** column shows:

| Badge | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Private** | Only you |
| **Organization** | Everyone in the organization |
| **Shared · 1 person, 2 teams** | Specific people and teams |
| **Shared with you** | Someone shared it with you |

A row you can see but not change also reads **Can use · not editable**, so you know before you open it.

## Deleting

Deleting asks for confirmation and warns you it can't be undone.

**Work already created from a template is never affected.** Applied work is ordinary items and workstreams with no live link back to the template — deleting the template leaves every item it ever created exactly where it is.
