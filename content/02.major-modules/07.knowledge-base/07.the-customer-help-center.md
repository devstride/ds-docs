## What customers call it

In the Service Desk portal, the Knowledge Base is presented as the **Help center**. Same articles, customer-facing wording, and a deliberately narrower selection.

## How customers reach it

Signed-in portal users see a **Help center** link on their **My requests** page. It opens at:

```
/portal/<your-portal-slug>/kb
```

An individual article lives at:

```
/portal/<your-portal-slug>/kb/<article-slug>
```

::alert{type="info" title="The portal slug belongs to the company, not your organization"}
Each customer company has its own portal address. An article shared with three companies is reachable at three different URLs — one per company — which is why the editor confirms an article is **Live on your customer portal** rather than printing a single link.

Company identity comes from the customer's signed-in session, never from the URL. A customer cannot see another company's articles by editing the address.
::

Customers must be **signed in** to the portal to read the Help center. It is not a public, anonymous documentation site.

## What customers see

Only articles that pass every gate:

- **Published** — drafts and archived articles never appear.
- **Visibility: Portal (customers)** — internal articles never appear.
- **No Internal category above them** — a hidden section takes its contents with it.
- **Shared with their company** — either *All companies*, or a list including theirs.

Everything else is genuinely absent: not in the category tree, not in search results, and the direct URL returns "not found".

Uncategorized articles are grouped under **General**.

## Browsing and searching

The Help center opens on **Browse articles**, showing the categories and articles available to that company in **the order you curated** on the Manage surface. Your arrangement reaches customers — the most important article in a section is the one you put first, not the one that happens to start with "A".

The search box is labelled **Search articles**, with the placeholder *Search the help center…*. Results appear under **Search results**, ranked exactly as they are for your team — exact title matches first, then title-prefix, then title-contains, then body relevance.

Customer-facing messages:

| Situation | What they see |
|---|---|
| Nothing published for them | *No articles have been published yet.* |
| Search found nothing | *No articles match your search.* |
| Could not load | *We could not load the help center. Please try again.* with a **Try again** button |
| Article failed to load | *We could not load this article. Please try again.* |

Navigation links read **Back to help center** and **Back to my requests**.

## Article suggestions on the request form

The Help center also works to *prevent* tickets. When a customer is submitting a request, the form searches your published articles as they describe their problem:

- Prompt: **What do you need help with?**
- Placeholder: *Describe your problem…*
- Matches appear under **These articles may help**
- If nothing matches: *No matching articles — go ahead and submit your request.*

This is the highest-value place your Knowledge Base pays for itself. A customer who finds the answer here never files the ticket.

::alert{type="tip" title="Write for the words customers actually use"}
Suggestions match on titles and body text, with titles weighted more heavily. A customer types *"can't log in"*, not *"authentication failure remediation"*. Title your customer-facing articles the way customers describe the problem, and the deflection search will find them.

If your support team keeps answering the same question, that question is a title.
::

## Testing what customers see

You cannot fully verify the customer experience from the authoring surface, because the Library deliberately shows your team everything.

1. **Quick check** — in the article editor, look for **Live on your customer portal** under the slug. If it is there, the article passes the state, visibility, and category gates. It does not tell you about the *company* list.
2. **Company check** — the **Companies** summary in the editor's **Audience** panel. **All companies**, or the specific list.
3. **Full check** — open the portal as a customer of the relevant company and browse the Help center. This is the only way to see exactly what they see, including ordering and category structure.

::alert{type="warning" title="Watch for No companies"}
An article set to **Specific companies** with every company removed is visible to nobody, while still reading as `published` and `portal`. The editor flags it — *"No companies selected — this article is not visible to any portal company."* — and the Manage table styles the **No companies** value as a warning. If an article is mysteriously invisible to every customer, check this first.
::

## Getting the most out of it

**Start with your ticket queue.** The articles worth writing first are the answers your team is already typing repeatedly. Look at your most common request subjects and write those.

**Put the customer-facing sections first.** Category order is yours to set, and customers read top-down.

**Use Internal categories for the working half.** Keep runbooks, escalation procedures, and internal notes in an Internal section. Your team finds them in the Library alongside everything else; customers never know they exist.

**Stage a section before launch.** Build a new section under an Internal parent category, get every article right, then flip the parent to Portal. Everything goes live at once, already configured — and if you need to pull it back, flipping it to Internal restores exactly the state you had.
