## What History records

Every article keeps a history. Each entry is a snapshot of the article **after** a change, stamped with **who** made it and **when**, so you can answer "who edited this, and what did it say before?" without keeping copies of your own.

You will find it in the **History** panel of the editor rail, directly under **Status & lifecycle**. Entries are listed newest first: the top one is the article as it stands now and is marked **Current**; the oldest is its creation.

Each entry says what kind of change it was:

| Entry | What happened |
|---|---|
| **Created** | The article was created. Every history starts here. |
| **Edited** | The title or body was saved. |
| **Restored** | An earlier version was restored. |
| **Published** / **Unpublished** | The article was made live, or pulled back to draft. |
| **Archived** / **Unarchived** | The article was retired, or brought back to draft. |
| **Audience changed** | Visibility or the company audience changed. |

A change that changes nothing is not recorded. Re-publishing an article that is already published, or saving the Audience panel without altering the selection, leaves no entry — history reports what actually happened, not what was clicked.

::alert{type="note" title="A writing session is one entry, not fifty"}
Title and body autosave shortly after you stop typing, so a long edit would otherwise produce dozens of entries. Instead, consecutive **Edited** entries by the same person within **15 minutes** are grouped into a single entry, dated at the **last** save in that run. A pause longer than that, somebody else editing, or any non-edit action starts the next entry.
::

## Reading the panel

Each row shows the kind of change, who made it, and how long ago. The newest row carries the **Current** badge; every older row has a **Preview** button. If there is more history than fits, **Load more** fetches the next page.

If the history cannot be loaded — a network or server problem — the panel says so and offers **Retry**, and keeps showing whatever it already had. It never reports an empty history when it simply failed to fetch one.

## Previewing an earlier version

**Preview** on any older row opens that version: its kind, who made it, when, its title, and its body rendered read-only. Nothing has changed at this point — you are looking at a record, and closing the dialog leaves the article exactly as it was.

## Restoring a version

From the preview, **Restore this version** puts that version's content back.

**A restore changes the title and body, and nothing else.** Specifically it leaves alone:

- the publish state — restoring a published article edits it live, in place, and does not unpublish it
- the URL slug
- visibility and the company audience
- the category

**Nothing is deleted.** The restore is added as a *new* **Restored** entry at the top of the history, pointing at the version it came from. Everything that happened after that version is still listed — so a restore can itself be undone by restoring the entry just before it.

::alert{type="caution" title="Restoring a published article is a live edit"}
There is no draft step. If the article is published and customer-facing, your readers see the restored content as soon as it saves. Preview first; if you want to review before customers do, **Unpublish** the article, restore, then republish.
::

## Who can do what

Viewing history needs nothing beyond the access that opens the article — if you can read it in the editor, you can read its history.

Restoring is an **edit**, so it needs exactly the same permission editing the article's content would:

| The article | To restore a version you need |
|---|---|
| Your own **draft** | **Create knowledge-base articles** |
| Your own **published or archived** article | **Edit knowledge-base articles you authored**, or **Publish knowledge-base articles** |
| **Someone else's** article | **Edit any member's knowledge-base articles** |

Without those, the preview opens read-only and offers no Restore. An article you are not allowed to see returns "not found" — and so does its history, for the same reason: an error that distinguished the two would confirm the article exists.

See [Knowledge Base permissions](/major-modules/knowledge-base/knowledge-base-permissions) for the full picture.

## Two things worth knowing

**Existing articles already have a history.** When this feature shipped, every article that already existed was given its **Created** entry, so no history starts empty.

**An entry can be attributed to DevStride.** Very occasionally an entry reads **Changed outside history**, attributed to *DevStride* rather than a person. That is a repair: the article was found to differ from its newest entry, so the content that was actually there was recorded rather than quietly dropped. It is normal immediately around a release and needs no action — the entry preserves what the article said at that moment.
