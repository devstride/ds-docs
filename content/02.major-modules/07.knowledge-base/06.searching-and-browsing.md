## Two ways to find an article

**Browsing** — pick a category from the tree and read what is in it, in the order you curated. Good when the reader knows roughly where to look.

**Searching** — type words into the search box. Good when they know what they want but not where it lives.

Both are available on all three surfaces: your team's Library, the Manage authoring list, and the customer Help center.

## Browsing by category

Selecting a category shows the articles in it **and everything in its subcategories**. Selecting a top-level product category gives you the whole product's content, not just the handful of articles filed directly at the top.

**All articles** clears the category filter. **Uncategorized** shows articles with no category at all.

Browse order is the one you curated by dragging rows on the Manage surface — for your team and your customers alike. See [Organizing with categories](/major-modules/knowledge-base/organizing-with-categories).

## Searching

The search box carries the placeholder **Search articles** and is clearable. It searches as you type, with a short pause so it fires when you stop rather than on every keystroke.

Search covers **titles and body text**.

### How results are ranked

Results are ordered by how well they match, strongest first:

1. **The title is exactly your search** — ranked highest.
2. **The title starts with your search.**
3. **The title contains your search.**
4. **Everything else** — body-text relevance, with title matches weighted more heavily than body matches.

Ties are broken alphabetically by title.

The practical effect: searching *password* puts an article titled "Password" first, then "Password reset", then "Reset a forgotten password", then articles that merely mention passwords in the body. Partial words match from the start, so *pass* finds *password*.

Ranking works identically on all three surfaces, so what your customers experience matches what you see when you test a search.

::alert{type="note" title="Symbol-only searches return nothing"}
A search containing only punctuation has nothing to match on, so it returns an empty result with the message **Nothing to search for** — *"That search only contains symbols. Try words or numbers instead."* It deliberately does not fall back to showing everything, which would look like a broken search.
::

## Filters on the Manage surface

The authoring list adds a state filter your readers never see: **All**, **Draft**, **Published**, **Archived**.

There is one subtlety worth knowing:

- **Browsing** with **All** selected shows drafts and published articles — the *active* list. Archived articles are not included.
- **Searching** with **All** selected likewise shows drafts and published articles and **excludes archived**.

Neither browsing nor searching should resurface content you retired. When you genuinely want to find an archived article, select the **Archived** chip explicitly.

If a category (or the whole Knowledge Base) has archived articles but nothing active, the list says **No active articles to show** and points you at the Archived filter, rather than inviting you to write a first article that already exists.

Drafts are filtered per reader: you always see your own, and holders of **Publish knowledge-base articles** or **Manage all knowledge-base articles** see everyone's.

### The Manage table columns

| Column | Shows |
|---|---|
| **Title** | The article title |
| **State** | `draft`, `published`, or `archived` |
| **Visibility** | The article's **own** setting — `portal` or `internal` |
| **Audience** | `All companies`, `N companies`, `No companies`, or `Internal` |
| **Category** | Where it is filed |
| **Author** | Who created it |
| **Updated** | Relative time, e.g. "3 days ago" |

::alert{type="caution" title="The Visibility column is not the effective answer"}
It shows what the article is set to, not what a customer would experience. An article reading `portal` here can still be hidden by an Internal category above it. For the effective answer, check the **Internal** badge on the category tree or the **Live on your customer portal** line in the editor.
::

## What each surface includes

| | Team Library | Manage list | Customer Help center |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drafts | Never | Yours, plus all with permission | Never |
| Published, internal | Yes, badged **Internal** | Yes | Never |
| Published, portal | Yes | Yes | If category and company allow |
| Archived | Never | Under the **Archived** filter | Never |
| Company audience applies | No | No | Yes |
| Category audience applies | No | No | Yes |

Your team's Library ignores the customer-facing gates entirely — members see everything published. That is the point: your support team should be able to find an internal runbook and a customer-facing guide in the same search.

## Result limits

Lists are capped rather than paginated:

- **Manage list** — 200 articles
- **Team Library** — 50 articles
- **Customer Help center** — 50 articles

In practice you reach these by browsing **All articles** in a large knowledge base. Narrow by category or search rather than scrolling.

## When nothing comes back

Each surface distinguishes four situations, so the message tells you what to do:

| Situation | Headline | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing written yet | **No articles yet** | The organization has no articles at all. |
| Empty category | **No articles in this category yet** | The category exists but nothing is filed in it. |
| No matches | **No results for "widget"** | The search ran and found nothing. |
| Unsearchable input | **Nothing to search for** | The search was only symbols. |
| Could not load | **Articles could not be loaded** | A network or server problem — use **Try again**. |

The last one is not an empty result. If you see it, the list is unknown rather than empty, and the article count is withheld rather than shown as zero.
